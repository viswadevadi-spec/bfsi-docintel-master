# RBI lending guidance — retrieval demo

This file is a retrieval-oriented summary of the specific RBI provisions cited
in each section. It is not legal advice or a substitute for checking the
current direction, amendments, lender type, product, and borrower facts.

The 50% household repayment limit and the Key Facts Statement provisions below
are from the RBI's *Regulatory Framework for Microfinance Loans Directions,
2022*, updated on the RBI page through July 17, 2025. They apply in the
microfinance context described by that direction; do not generalize them to
every retail loan without checking the applicable rules.

# Section 2.1 - Applicability of the Microfinance Direction
Summary: The Direction identifies the regulated entities to which its microfinance framework applies.
Source: RBI Master Direction, Regulatory Framework for Microfinance Loans Directions, 2022, paragraphs 2.1-2.2. https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12256
Para 2.1: The Direction applies to specified commercial banks (excluding Payments Banks), Primary (Urban) Co-operative Banks, State Co-operative Banks, District Central Co-operative Banks, and Non-Banking Financial Companies, including microfinance institutions and housing finance companies.
Para 2.2: The entities listed in paragraph 2.1 are referred to as Regulated Entities for purposes of this Direction.

# Section 3.2 - Meaning of a Microfinance Loan
Summary: The Direction defines microfinance loans by collateral-free status and the household's annual income threshold.
Source: RBI Master Direction, Regulatory Framework for Microfinance Loans Directions, 2022, paragraph 3.2. https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12256
Para 3.2: A microfinance loan is a collateral-free loan to a household with annual household income up to ₹3,00,000. The Direction's definition covers loans to low-income households irrespective of end use, delivery channel, or the lender's classification, subject to the terms in the Direction.

# Section 3.4 - Repayment Periodicity
Summary: Microfinance repayment frequency should be flexible to the borrower's requirements; this provision does not prescribe a universal minimum loan tenor.
Source: RBI Master Direction, Regulatory Framework for Microfinance Loans Directions, 2022, paragraph 3.4. https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12256
Para 3.4: A Regulated Entity must have a board-approved policy providing flexibility in repayment periodicity for microfinance loans according to borrowers' requirements. This paragraph concerns repayment frequency and does not state a general minimum loan tenure.

# Section 4.1 - Assessment of Household Income
Summary: Regulated Entities must establish a board-approved policy for assessing household income.
Source: RBI Master Direction, Regulatory Framework for Microfinance Loans Directions, 2022, paragraph 4.1. https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12256
Para 4.1: Each Regulated Entity must put in place a board-approved policy for assessment of household income. The Direction provides an indicative methodology in Annex I.

# Section 5.1 - Household Loan Repayment Obligation Limit
Summary: For microfinance household underwriting, aggregate monthly loan repayments are capped at 50% of monthly household income.
Source: RBI Master Direction, Regulatory Framework for Microfinance Loans Directions, 2022, paragraphs 5.1-5.4. https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12256
Para 5.1: Each Regulated Entity must have a board-approved policy limiting household monthly loan repayment outflows to a maximum of 50% of monthly household income.
Para 5.2: The calculation includes outstanding collateral-free microfinance loans and other collateralized loans. It includes principal and interest repayments on existing loans and the loan being considered.
Para 5.3: Existing loans above the 50% limit may mature, but no new loans may be provided to the household until the prescribed limit is met.
Para 5.4: Regulated Entities must provide accurate household-income data to Credit Information Companies, use available credit information, and ascertain indebtedness through other sources.

# Section 6.1 - Pricing of Microfinance Loans
Summary: Pricing must be governed by a board-approved policy; the RBI Direction does not set a single universal interest rate.
Source: RBI Master Direction, Regulatory Framework for Microfinance Loans Directions, 2022, paragraphs 6.1-6.2. https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12256
Para 6.1: The board-approved pricing policy must cover the interest-rate model, components such as cost of funds and risk premium, component ranges for borrower categories, and a ceiling on interest rates and other applicable charges.
Para 6.2: Interest rates and other charges or fees on microfinance loans must not be usurious and are subject to RBI supervisory scrutiny.

# Section 6A - Key Facts Statement for Microfinance Loans
Summary: Prospective microfinance borrowers receive an understandable KFS; its validity window depends on loan tenor, and it discloses APR and repayment information.
Source: RBI Master Direction, Regulatory Framework for Microfinance Loans Directions, 2022, paragraphs 6A.2-6A.7. https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12256
Para 6A.2: Before the loan contract is executed, the Regulated Entity must provide each prospective borrower a standardized KFS in a language the borrower understands, explain it, and obtain acknowledgement of understanding.
Para 6A.3: The KFS must have a unique proposal number and remain valid for at least three working days for loans with a tenor of seven days or more, and one working day for loans with a tenor below seven days. This is the borrower's time to accept the disclosed terms, not a minimum loan-tenure rule.
Para 6A.4: The KFS must include the APR computation and the loan amortization schedule. APR includes charges levied by the Regulated Entity.
Para 6A.5: Applicable third-party charges recovered by the Regulated Entity must be included in APR, disclosed separately, and supported by receipts and related documents.
Para 6A.6: A fee or charge absent from the KFS cannot be charged during the loan term without the borrower's explicit consent.
Para 6A.7: The KFS must also appear as a summary box in the loan agreement.

# Section 12 - Housing Loan-to-Value Ratios (Historical RBI Circular)
Summary: A 2013 RBI Master Circular for banks recorded housing-loan LTV tiers of 90%, 80%, and 75% by loan amount. This historical, housing-specific circular is not a universal or current LTV rule for all retail lending.
Source: RBI Master Circular - Housing Finance, July 1, 2013, paragraph 12. https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/english/scripts/Notification.aspx?Id=1269
Para 12.1: In the cited 2013 circular, individual housing loans up to ₹20 lakh had a 90% LTV ceiling; loans above ₹20 lakh and up to ₹75 lakh had an 80% ceiling; and loans above ₹75 lakh had a 75% ceiling. The circular also said stamp duty, registration, and documentation charges should not be included in the financed property's value for the LTV calculation.
Para 12.2: The cited tiers are housing-loan-specific and from a 2013 circular. They do not establish an 85% universal RBI cap. Confirm the currently applicable RBI direction and lender category before using an LTV limit for a real decision.
